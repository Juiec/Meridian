# Meridian — Design Context

## Users
- **Single, technically capable owner-operator** running their own model (typically LM Studio, often over a tailnet). They chose a local-first stack on purpose: sessions, skills, memory files, and settings stay on disk; the browser never dials their tailnet directly. Privacy and control are assumptions, not features to explain.
- **Context of use**: long, uninterrupted working sessions in a browser tab. Chat is the primary surface, with Image Studio, Travel Planner, and Settings/Desk as secondary tools. The user moves between surfaces often and expects state to persist.
- **Job to be done**: think with a local model, direct it with skills and tools, and inspect/steer what it is doing without leaving the app. They are comfortable with dense technical detail and want it available, not hidden.
- **Emotional goal**: feel *capable* — a power tool under their control, not a companion or a toy. The interface should make status, limits, and state legible at a glance.

## Brand Personality
- **3 words**: Editorial, considered, crafted.
- **Voice**: Direct, plain, no filler. Match reply length to the weight of the ask. No "Great question", no restating the request, no play-by-play of tool calls already visible in the transcript.
- **Tone**: Confident without being loud. Warm without being cozy. The app reads like a well-made instrument with a point of view — not a generic AI chat window.
- **Name cue**: "Meridian" — a reference line, a waypoint. Navigation and orientation are natural motifs.

## Aesthetic Direction
- **Evolve, don't replace**: keep the established foundation — warm-neutral oklch palette (hue ~60–90), copper primary accent and sage secondary, Fraunces display type with Geist sans/mono, dark-first with a light toggle, ~0.625rem radius, shadcn `base-nova` primitives.
- **Push bolder and more distinctive**: increase contrast and typographic scale; let the Fraunces display face carry real hierarchy instead of decorating headers. Stronger accent identity, more deliberate composition. Aim to be remembered, not to blend in.
- **Editorial structure, power-tool density**: magazine-like clarity of hierarchy applied to information-dense surfaces. Three-column shell (sidebar / primary / inspector) and split-screen Travel Planner are the spatial signature. Grouping, rules, and spacing do the organizing — not a grid of identical cards.
- **Anti-references**: generic AI-slop aesthetics — cyan-on-dark glow, purple-blue gradients, everything-in-a-rounded-card, neon-on-black, Inter/Roboto defaults, meaningless sparklines, glassmorphism for its own sake. The product wireframe is intentionally low-fidelity and dark/warm-toned; treat that as origin, not final destination.
- **Theme**: dark by default (system follows stored `meridian-theme`, falls back to dark). Both themes must feel authored, not one inverted.

## Design Principles
1. **Editorial hierarchy, dense but scannable.** Type scale, spacing rhythm, and rules carry the structure. Surface more information, not less — but make the primary thing unmistakable.
2. **Bold intent in every choice.** Distinctive, authored decisions over safe defaults. If a layout could belong to any AI app, it is wrong.
3. **State is visible; chrome is quiet.** Streaming, tool calls, model/endpoint status, memory meters, and errors must be legible at a glance. Secondary controls use progressive disclosure; no repeated information.
4. **Accessibility is a floor, not a feature.** Meet WCAG AA contrast, honor `prefers-reduced-motion`, and support full keyboard navigation in both themes.
5. **Honest local-first framing.** Never invent data the app did not fetch (weather, fares, coordinates). Distinguish illustrative from live. Keep self-hosted/private framing plain and unpretentious.
